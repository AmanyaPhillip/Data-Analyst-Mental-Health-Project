## The project can ask something more analytical and prevention-oriented:
"What patterns and factors are associated with mental health challenges across populations, and how can data help organizations identify areas where mental-health support may be needed?"

## Possible KPI categories:
For example, depending on what the WHO dataset actually contains:
#### Mental-health prevalence
- Mental health disorder prevalence
- Depression prevalence
- Anxiety prevalence
- Mental health burden per 100,000 population
#### Population
- Number of people affected
 -Percentage of population affected
- Age-group distribution
- Gender distribution, if available
#### Geographic
- Highest/lowest prevalence by country
- Regional average
- Change by country over time
- Countries with the largest increase/decrease
#### Time
- Year-over-year change
- Long-term trend
- Percentage change from baseline year
- Peak year / lowest year
#### ResourceMental-health professionals per population
- Mental-health expenditure
- Treatment/access indicators
- Service availability / service indicators — only if the WHO dataset contains them
## Dashboard KPIs
#### You could eventually have cards such as:
- Global Mental Health Rate
- Year-over-Year Change
- Highest-Affected Region
- Lowest-Affected Region
- Population Affected
- Countries Showing Increasing Trends

But we should only finalize KPIs after looking at the actual WHO dataset. We don't want to invent KPIs that the data cannot support.
